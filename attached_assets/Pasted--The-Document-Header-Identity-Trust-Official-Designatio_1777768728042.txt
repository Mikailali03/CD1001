. The Document Header (Identity & Trust)
Official Designation: Top-left corner clearly states "Official Property Dossier" in a small, wide-tracked monospace font.
The Address: The primary street address is the largest text on the page, set in a heavy, black sans-serif font to ground the document.
The "Verified" Seal: On the top-right, a thick-bordered "Verified" badge with a ShieldCheck icon. This is styled with a heavy offset shadow to mimic a physical rubber stamp, signaling that the data has been audited via the Casa Dossier protocol.
2. Technical Specifications Strip
Below the header is a four-column grid that acts as the "Quick Specs" for the building:
Built: The year of original construction.
Structure: Total square footage.
Configuration: Bedroom and Bathroom count.
Registry: The total count of audited service events (e.g., "42 Audited Events").
Design Detail: Each stat is separated by a clean vertical line (border-l), making it feel like an architectural blueprint.
3. Current Operational Inventory (Active Assets)
This is the "meat" of the report. Every active system (HVAC, Water Heater, etc.) has its own dedicated block.
System Header: Shows the Sub-category (e.g., Tankless Water Heater) in bold caps, with the Brand and Model beneath it.
Technical Spec Badge: On the right of each asset header, a black badge shows the Manufacture Date and Serial Number.
Chronological Service Table:
A clean table lists the Date, the Service Item (e.g., "Annual Flush"), and the Performer (e.g., "Main Street Plumbing").
Privacy Guard: Cost data is explicitly excluded. This allows the owner to prove the work was done without revealing what they paid, which is ideal for negotiations.
Visual Logic: Each asset uses a break-inside-avoid CSS rule, ensuring that a system and its history table are never split across two different physical pages.
4. Property Systems Archive (Decommissioned Assets)
Following the active systems is the Archive. This is what sets Casa Dossier apart from a simple list of appliances.
Visual Distinction: This section is slightly faded (opacity-70) to show it is historical data.
The "Death Certificate": Every retired asset has a high-visibility badge: "DECOMMISSIONED [DATE]".
The Narrative: This section proves a "Chain of Care." It shows the buyer, for example, that the previous AC unit was serviced regularly for 15 years before being replaced by the current one.
5. Authentication Footer
Generation Timestamp: A clear line stating exactly when the report was generated.
Ecosystem Verification: Three icons (Wrench, ShieldCheck, FileText) represent the three pillars of the report: Technical Specification, Verified Logic, and Chain of Custody.
Protocol Note: Mentions the "Casa Dossier Protocol," adding a layer of professional authority to the document.
